# CraftID governance note — HCU / EUFUA relationship and certificate design

Date: 24 September 2026

## 1. Institutional relationship to preserve in CraftID

For CraftID governance and institutional positioning, HCU and EUFUA should not be treated as unrelated partner organisations.

### HCU

HCU (Handicraft Chamber of Ukraine / MSMEs–HCU) is the Ukrainian craft and MSME institutional base connected to the origin and sectoral development of CraftID.

### EUFUA

EUFUA is the European institutional interface and holds a full representative mandate for HCU in the European Union.

### Common founding continuity

HCU and EUFUA have the same founding persons behind the initiative. This is relevant as an internal governance and continuity fact, but it should not be used as the main public trust claim.

The public narrative should focus on institutional roles rather than founders:

- HCU — craft/MSME sectoral base and Ukrainian institutional counterpart;
- EUFUA — European representation, institutional interface and coordination bridge;
- CraftID — the professional identity infrastructure itself.

This relationship must not be described in a way that implies an EU institutional mandate, EU endorsement, statutory authority, or public-law status unless separately documented.

Any public wording about the EUFUA representative mandate must be based on owner-approved documentary evidence and should state exactly what the mandate covers.

## 2. Network / partner model implication

CraftID should preserve the distinction between:

- project / infrastructure governance;
- national operator roles;
- network partners;
- institutional representation.

HCU and EUFUA may therefore have governance or founding roles that are not equivalent to ordinary Partner records.

Do not force them into a generic partner category if that obscures their actual relationship to CraftID.

## 3. Certificate design — mandatory revisit

The terminology correction from “Identity Certificate” to “CraftID Record Certificate” is only the first step.

The certificate must be redesigned as a dedicated institutional artefact.

The next certificate design pass should cover:

- visual hierarchy;
- exact certificate title;
- CraftID number prominence;
- Certificate No. placement;
- QR placement and verification logic;
- Professional vs Workshop variants;
- EN / UA versions;
- use of the CraftID mark;
- issuer / infrastructure attribution;
- issue date and version;
- legal/disclaimer language;
- relationship between the certificate and the live public CraftID record;
- print-safe A4 layout;
- optional round sticker / mark design as a separate artefact;
- distinction between certificate, mark, downloadable profile card and workshop signage.

Important rule:

- Certificate No. appears only on the certificate.
- Other downloadable or printable CraftID materials normally show only the CraftID number and QR code.

The certificate must not visually imitate a statutory qualification certificate, professional licence, accreditation, or EU-issued credential.

The design should communicate:
“this is an authenticated versioned record artefact from CraftID”
rather than:
“this person has been professionally certified.”

## 4. Next action

Return to certificate design after the current architecture/content remediation pass and prepare a dedicated design brief before producing final templates.
